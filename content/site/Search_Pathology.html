Pathology in game-trees is a counterintuitive phenomenon where a deeper minimax search results in worse play. It was discovered by Don Beal 1, who constructed a simple mathematical model to analyze the minimax algorithm. To his surprise, the analysis of the model showed that the backed-up values were actually somewhat less trustworthy than the heuristic values themselves.

Decision vs Evaluation

Independently, pathology in game trees was coined by Dana S. Nau, who discovered pathology to exist in a large class of games 2 under the assumption of independence of sibling values of trees with low branching factor (i.e. binary trees) with game theoretic leaf values of win and loss {1, -1}. In a simulation, Nau introduced strong dependencies between sibling nodes and discovered that this can cause search-depth pathology to disappear. While Nau was primarily concerned with decision accuracy, Beal 3, as well as Bratko and Gams 4 were concerned with evaluation accuracy.

Random Evaluations

Quite contrary to pathology in chess, Beal and others demonstrated 5, that deeper search with random leaf values yields to better play. As a quintessence, Dana S. Nau et al. suggested an Error minimizing minimax search in their recent paper 6 .
