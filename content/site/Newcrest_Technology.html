CXG Newcrest Technology Ltd., (CXG Systems)
a Hong Kong based manufacturer of consumer electronics and dedicated chess computers managed by Australian technologist Eric White along with Ken Cohen. CXG forerunner were founded in 1979 as Cassia and in 1981 as White and Allcock 1, renamed in 1984 to CXG Newcrest Technology Limited, in 1991 changed over to National Telecommunications System Ltd. with further successors Krypton Ltd. (1994) and Timorite Ltd. (1995-1996), Systema Leisure Ltd. (1997-2001), and subsequently other Hong Kong companies, continuing the dedicated computer business 2.

Cassia

The first dedicated chess computer of Cassia was the Cassia Chess Mate in 1979, a clone of Ron Nelson's Chess Challenger 10 3 4, also shipped as Toytronic Chess Electronics 5 6, and CXG Computachess I 7.

CXG

In 1981, Eric White started the collaboration with David Levy's and Kevin O’Connell's new company Intelligent Software. White and Allcock's first own dedicated chess computer was the CXG Sensor Computachess 8 with a 4-bit [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachi_Ltd. Hitachi] processor HD44801, and the program by Mark Taylor using only 160 nibbles of RAM, which predecessor was already instantiated in the SciSys Mini Chess series earlier this year. CXG became brand name of White and Allcock and since 1984 CXG Newcrest Technology Ltd.

Chess computers were designed in Hong Kong, manufactured in China, and then marketed under various brand names and retailers, including CXG 9 10, Computer Games Ltd. (CGL), ComputaChess, Crown, Hanimex, Unimex, Tandy via RadioShack, Krypton 11 12, Schneider 13 14, Systema and Toytronic. In 1998 in partnership with Tiger Electronics (Hasbro) 15, White produced the Tiger Grenadier and Tiger Voice Master 16. Chess computers were also manufactured for Excalibur and Millennium 2000 17. David Levy was the source of programs for all cheaper models 18, using Sunplus 8-bit chips 19 (now Generalplus 20).
